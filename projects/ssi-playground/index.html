Interactive SSI-cov

Covariance-driven stochastic subspace identification of a simulated girder with known modal properties, computed in the browser.

A web application for studying the accuracy of covariance-driven stochastic subspace identification (SSI-cov). The natural frequencies and damping ratios of a simply supported girder, the number of sensors, the measurement noise, the record length and the identification settings are set by the user. The application simulates the acceleration response to white-noise excitation, identifies the modes with SSI-cov and automatic mode selection, and compares the results with the true values. The computations are implemented in Python and NumPy and run client-side through Pyodide; the figures are rendered directly as SVG.

  • Stabilization diagram, mode shapes, and a comparison of identified and true natural frequencies, damping ratios and MAC values. One identification takes about 0.5 s.
  • Repeated identification over independent records quantifies the bias of the damping estimate. For the mode with 3.5 % damping, the mean error over 21 records was −0.39 percentage points with 30 block rows and −0.83 with 120 block rows.
  • The bias decreases with record length, from −1.2 to −0.2 percentage points between 30 and 180 minutes at 120 block rows, which identifies it as a finite-record effect. The same bias was corrected in the damping estimates of the Hardanger Bridge analysis.
  • Each mode is simulated exactly in discrete time, so its true frequency and damping are known. Unit tests verify the identification against these values.
