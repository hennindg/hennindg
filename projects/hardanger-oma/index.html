Operational Modal Analysis of the Hardanger Bridge

An automatic pipeline that turns 1,485 thirty-minute acceleration recordings of a 1310 m suspension bridge into natural frequencies, damping ratios and mode shapes – and uses them to study wind effects and damage detection.

The Hardanger Bridge is instrumented with 20 accelerometers and 9 anemometers. I wrote the full analysis chain from scratch in Python: covariance-driven stochastic subspace identification (SSI-cov), automatic picking of stable poles by clustering, mode tracking across four years of data (2013–2018), regression on wind and temperature, and statistical novelty detection. Every step is validated – against simulated structures with known properties, against an independent identification from the literature, and against changes in its own settings.

  • Fully automatic identification: ~25 modes per recording (0.05–0.95 Hz) with no manual input; frequencies agree with an independent study (Petersen & Øiseth 2017) within −1.2 to +0.3 %.
  • Wind and temperature separated: frequencies drop 0.02–0.07 % per °C; vertical frequencies rise with U² – traced to the static wind load on the cable system.
  • Aerodynamic damping at full scale: the damping of all 13 vertical modes collapses onto one curve against reduced velocity, close to the quasi-steady wind-tunnel prediction.
  • Damage detection limits: after removing environmental effects, a Hotelling T² chart detects a uniform 1.1 % frequency drop in 90 % of single recordings – about a 3.5 % loss of cable stiffness, while local girder damage would go unnoticed.
  • Checking the method itself: found with simulations that SSI-cov underestimates high damping on 30-minute records, and built a calibrated simulated twin of every recording to correct it.
